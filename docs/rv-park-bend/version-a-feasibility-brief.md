# Bend / Deschutes County RV Park
## Development Feasibility & Market Analysis — **Version A: Institutional Brief**

*Prepared for: All Risk Uniforms / project sponsor*
*Date: August 2026*
*Status: Screening-stage analysis. Figures are market-derived estimates, not quotes. Items marked **[VERIFY]** require a local professional before any capital is committed.*

---

## 1. Executive Summary

A 20-site full-hookup RV park in the Bend market is **feasible as a ~$1.0–1.6M project, not as a $200,000 project.** The demand case is strong and the site economics work — but only above a scale threshold this plan currently sits below.

Three findings drive the recommendation:

| # | Finding | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Twenty sites is the worst possible scale.** Entitlement, well, septic backbone, power service extension and bathhouse are near-identical for 20 sites or 45. | Spreading fixed cost over 20 pads destroys the development margin. **Target 35–45 sites.** |
| 2 | **On EFU land, per-site hookups are illegal — permanently.** OAR 660-033-0130(19) bars separate sewer, water or electric hookups to individual campsites. | The cheap land ($3–6K/acre) and the full-hookup product are **mutually exclusive**. Phasing primitive→full hookup does not work on farm ground. |
| 3 | **$200,000 is an equity injection, not a budget.** SBA 7(a) finances ground-up RV park construction at roughly 10% down. | $200K supports a **$1.5–2.0M project**. This is the unlock, and it makes the original ambition achievable. |

**Recommendation: proceed to a 60-day feasibility gate on a 35–45 site full-hookup park, capitalized with SBA 7(a) construction debt against a $200K equity injection.** Do not option land until Gate 1 (§9) is cleared.

## 3. Demand Analysis

The demand case is the strongest part of this project and it holds up under scrutiny.

- **Bend transient room tax collections reached $14.5M in FY 2024–25, up 1.4%**, with hotel occupancy up 3%. Growth has moderated from post-pandemic peaks but remains positive.
- **Oregon tourism hit $14.6B in 2025**, with Bend adventure tourism specifically cited as a primary growth driver, including record winter-sports spending.
- The **US RV park and campground industry reached ~$10.9B in 2025.**
- Bend's demand is **year-round but strongly seasonal**: summer (Cascade Lakes, Deschutes River, trails) and winter (Mt. Bachelor) both draw, with weak shoulder seasons in November and April.

**Underwriting position:** model annual occupancy at **46–52%**, not the 65–70% that promotional pro formas assume. Central Oregon winters are real. A park that pencils at 48% is a park that survives.

## 4. Competitive Supply

The market is tiered, and the tiers price very differently.

| Tier | Representative | Sites | Peak nightly | Position |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Premium resort | Crown Villa RV Resort | 100+ FHU | **$90–$136** | Clubhouse, hot tub, steam room, fitness centre, paved FHU sites, storage |
| Mid-market | Scandia RV Park | 60+ FHU | **$50+** | Family-owned, three Bend locations, reported near-continuous full occupancy |
| Public / dispersed | State and USFS campgrounds | — | $10–$35 | No hookups, seasonal, reservation-constrained |

**Read:** the spread between the mid-market and the premium tier is roughly **$60–$85 per site-night** — the single largest value lever available. That gap is bought with amenity, finish and site width, not with pad count. Scandia's reported occupancy also suggests the mid-market is supply-constrained, which is the more reliable signal.

**Implication:** enter at the premium end or don't enter. A 20-pad park competing on price against Scandia's 60 pads loses on cost structure. A boutique park at premium ADR is the only defensible position at small scale.

## 5. Regulatory Pathway — The Gating Item

Oregon has operated statewide land-use planning since 1973. Everything depends on the parcel's zone. **Site selection is the entire game.**

### 5.1 Zone eligibility

| Zone | RV park status | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| Rural Commercial (RC) | Conditional use | **Preferred target** |
| Rural Industrial (RI) | Conditional use | **Preferred target** |
| MUA-10 / RR-10 | Expansion of *existing* park only | Not viable for new build |
| EFU (Exclusive Farm Use) | "Private parks / campgrounds" permitted per ORS 215.283 — **but no per-site hookups** (OAR 660-033-0130(19)) | Viable only for the Version B strategy |
| Forest (F1/F2) | Private campground per ORS 215.459; yurts capped at 1/3 of sites or 10, whichever is smaller | Viable only for the Version B strategy |
| Destination Resort overlay | Requires 20+ acre tract on county-mapped eligible land | Out of scope at this capital level |

### 5.2 County standards — DCC 18.128.170

RV parks are a conditional use governed by **DCC 18.128.170**. Verified requirements include:

- **18.128.170(O): access must be from an arterial or collector street.** This is a hard filter and eliminates most cheap, remote parcels before any other analysis.
- Piped potable water and sewage disposal service to the park.
- Occupancy limited to **no more than 30 days in any 60-day period**.
- Toilets, lavatories and showers provided for each sex.
- Minimum roadway widths; spaces surfaced with crushed gravel, asphalt, concrete or similar.

**[VERIFY]** Obtain the current full text of DCC 18.128.170 and confirm minimum parcel size, density per acre, setbacks and screening directly with Deschutes County Planning (541-388-6560). The county has been actively amending RV park code — see file **247-25-000106-TA (Tumalo RV Park Text Amendment)**, whose proposed language requires parcels of **2–5 acres located within a sewer district** with highway-adjacent common ownership. Amendments in flight can help or hurt; confirm current status before optioning.

### 5.3 Full permit stack

| Permit | Agency | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Conditional Use Permit + Site Plan Review | Deschutes County CDD | Public hearing, findings of fact, **appealable by neighbours to LUBA** |
| On-site wastewater / WPCF permit | Oregon DEQ (OAR 340-071) | Septic feasibility test is a **go/no-go**; run it first |
| Water right or exempt-well determination | OWRD | See §5.4 — the sharpest constraint |
| Recreation park construction plan approval | DCBS **Building Codes Division** (OAR 918-650) | Required *prior to* construction |
| Annual operating licence | Oregon Health Authority (ORS 446.310–446.350, OAR 333-031) | Renewed annually |
| Road approach permit | County Road Dept or ODOT | Ties to the arterial/collector requirement |

**Timeline: 9–15 months. Soft costs: $55,000–$120,000.** Budget for an Oregon land-use attorney from day one; a LUBA appeal adds 6–12 months and is the most common way these projects die.

### 5.4 Water — the sharpest constraint

This is where Deschutes County projects fail, and it deserves specific attention.

- **Exempt commercial/industrial well use is capped at 5,000 gallons/day** (ORS 537.545(1)(f); OAR 690-340-0010), and **only one well system per ownership or tax lot**.
- RV park demand runs roughly 50–100 gal/site/day. At 100 gal/site/day, **5,000 gpd supports about 50 sites** — so a 35–45 site park *may* fit within the exemption. This is a genuinely favourable finding and materially de-risks the water line item.
- **But**: the **Deschutes Basin Groundwater Mitigation Program** requires mitigation credits (1 credit = 1 acre-foot) for new groundwater uses requiring a permit. If demand exceeds the exemption, mitigation credit cost enters the budget. **[VERIFY]** current credit pricing with OWRD or the Deschutes River Conservancy.
- **Drilling cost:** Oregon runs **$28–$65/foot**; Central Oregon wells near Bend commonly reach **200–400 feet**. Budget **$25,000–$70,000** all-in with pump, casing, storage and treatment.

**Action: confirm the exempt-well pathway before anything else.** If the project fits under 5,000 gpd, it avoids both a water right permit and mitigation credits. That single determination is worth more than any other early expenditure.

## 6. Development Cost Model

Ex-land, for a **20-site** full-hookup park:

| Line item | Low | High |
|---|---:|---:|
| Entitlement & soft costs (CUP, land-use counsel, civil engineering, traffic, survey, septic feasibility) | $55,000 | $120,000 |
| Clearing, grading, stormwater | $60,000 | $130,000 |
| Interior roads (gravel → paved) | $50,000 | $140,000 |
| Pads ×20 | $40,000 | $120,000 |
| Water: well, pump, storage, treatment, distribution | $70,000 | $180,000 |
| Wastewater: community septic / DEQ-approved system | $90,000 | $250,000 |
| Electrical service extension + 20× 50/30/20A pedestals | $80,000 | $220,000 |
| Bathhouse / office / laundry | $90,000 | $240,000 |
| Amenities, landscaping, signage, fencing | $25,000 | $70,000 |
| FF&E, reservation system, launch marketing | $15,000 | $40,000 |
| Contingency @ 15% | $87,000 | $200,000 |
| **Total ex-land** | **$662,000** | **$1,710,000** |
| **Per site** | **$33,100** | **$85,500** |

Land: **$120,000–$450,000** for 5–10 zoned, access-qualifying, water-capable acres **[VERIFY]**.

**Total project: $780,000 – $2,160,000.** Midpoint ≈ **$1.4M**.

> **The scale problem, stated plainly.** Of the ex-land total, roughly **$400,000–$900,000 is fixed backbone** — entitlement, well, septic, power service, bathhouse — that barely changes between 20 sites and 45. Only pads, pedestals and road length scale with count. Adding 25 sites costs perhaps **$18,000–$30,000 each** at the margin while generating full incremental revenue.

### 7.3 Capital structure

| Source | Amount | Notes |
|---|---:|---|
| Sponsor equity | $200,000 | The existing budget |
| SBA 7(a) construction loan | ~$1,800,000 | RV parks eligible under NAICS 721211; ground-up construction financed at ~10% down |
| **Total** | **~$2,000,000** | |

SBA 7(a) supports ground-up RV park and campground construction at approximately **10% down up to $5M**. SBA 504 is an alternative at ~20% down for larger fixed-asset projects.

**This is the central reframe of this brief.** The $200,000 was never the project budget. It is very close to the equity injection a lender will require on a $2M build. The original ambition is not too large — it was mis-scoped as an all-cash project.

**[VERIFY]** Lenders will require a full business plan with third-party feasibility study, personal guarantees, and typically some hospitality or operating experience. Engage an SBA-preferred lender familiar with outdoor hospitality early; their underwriting criteria should shape the plan, not the reverse.

## 9. Decision Gates

Sequenced so each gate costs less than the one after it, and any gate can kill the project cheaply.

**Gate 1 — Desk work (0–30 days, <$2,500)**
- Call Deschutes County Planning (541-388-6560); confirm current DCC 18.128.170 text and the status of 247-25-000106-TA
- Confirm the 5,000 gpd exempt-well pathway with OWRD
- Broker engagement: real pricing on RC / RI parcels with arterial or collector frontage
- Initial conversation with an SBA-preferred outdoor-hospitality lender
- *Kill criteria: no qualifying parcels available at a price supporting a $2M total project*

**Gate 2 — Site diligence (30–90 days, $15,000–$30,000)**
- Option 2–3 candidate parcels **subject to entitlement**
- Septic feasibility test
- Utility will-serve letters
- Pre-application conference with the county
- *Kill criteria: septic fails, or power extension exceeds $150,000*

**Gate 3 — Entitlement (90–330 days, $55,000–$120,000)**
- Civil engineering and site plan
- CUP application, hearing, findings
- Third-party feasibility study for the lender
- *Kill criteria: CUP denied and appeal prospects poor*

**Gate 4 — Capitalize and build (330 days+)**

## 10. Conclusion

The Bend market supports this asset. Demand is durable, the mid-market appears supply-constrained, and the premium tier prices at $90–$136 per site-night. The constraints are not demand-side.

**What must change from the original plan:**

1. **Scale up from 20 sites to 35–45.** At 20 sites the project builds for what it is worth. At 40 it generates a meaningful development spread. This is the single highest-leverage change available.
2. **Treat the $200,000 as equity, not budget.** SBA 7(a) turns it into a ~$2M project.
3. **Select the site on zone and road frontage first, price second.** RC or RI, arterial or collector access. Cheap EFU acreage cannot legally carry hookups, and the county requires qualifying access regardless of zone.
4. **Confirm water before anything else.** The exempt-well pathway is the difference between a manageable line item and a project-killer.

If the sponsor cannot or will not raise beyond $200,000 all-cash, **Version B is the applicable strategy** — a fundamentally different product built on the constraint rather than against it.
