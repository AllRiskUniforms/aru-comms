## Read this first: what the research changed

The original phone brief was directionally right on demand and wrong on cost and regulation. Five corrections matter enough to restate up front.

**1. Development cost was understated by 3–10x.**
The working figure was ~$5,000/site. Verified market cost is **$15,000–$50,000/site**, and **$40,000–$70,000** for a professionally engineered pad. A $100,000 development budget does not build 20 full-hookup sites anywhere, let alone in Central Oregon.

**2. On EFU land, per-site hookups are permanently prohibited.**
**OAR 660-033-0130(19)** bars separate sewer, water or electric hookups to individual campsites — electric to yurts is the only exception. The "start primitive, upgrade to full hookups later" plan **does not work on cheap farm ground.** This single rule forces the choice between the two versions, because it makes cheap land and the full-hookup product mutually exclusive.

**3. Twenty sites is the worst possible scale.**
Entitlement, well, septic backbone, power service and bathhouse cost roughly the same at 20 sites as at 45. Version A's model shows a 20-site park building for approximately what it is worth — **no development margin at all.** Fixing the scale is the highest-leverage change available to the conventional plan.

**4. $200,000 is an equity injection, not a project budget.**
SBA 7(a) finances ground-up RV park construction (NAICS 721211) at roughly **10% down**. That turns $200,000 into a ~$2M project. The original ambition was not too large — it was mis-scoped as an all-cash build.

**5. Cap rates are better than assumed.**
Class A/B parks are near **8.0% going-in** per Newmark's 2026 valuation survey, not 9.7%. Class C sits near 9.0%. Lower cap rates raise exit values roughly 15% against the original assumption.

Also corrected: the "~38 campgrounds serve the region" figure could not be substantiated and **should not appear in any lender-facing material.**

## The two questions that decide everything

Both are answerable in under two weeks for less than $500, and both should be answered **before any land is optioned.**

1. **Deschutes County Planning, 541-388-6560** — pre-application conference. Confirm the current text of **DCC 18.128.170**, the status of text amendment **247-25-000106-TA**, and, critically, **what access standard applies to your chosen zone.** DCC 18.128.170(O) requires arterial or collector access for RV parks; whether that reaches an EFU private campground is the highest-value open question in these documents.

2. **Oregon Water Resources Department** — confirm the project fits the **5,000 gallons/day commercial exempt-well ceiling** (ORS 537.545(1)(f); OAR 690-340-0010). Inside it, you need no water right and no Deschutes Basin mitigation credits. Outside it, water becomes the most dangerous line item in the budget.

## Regulatory pathway (applies to both versions)

| Permit | Agency | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| Conditional Use Permit + Site Plan Review | Deschutes County CDD | DCC 18.128.170 — appealable to LUBA |
| On-site wastewater / WPCF | Oregon DEQ | OAR 340-071 |
| Water right or exempt-well determination | OWRD | ORS 537.545, OAR 690-340-0010 |
| Recreation park construction approval | DCBS Building Codes Division | OAR 918-650 — required *before* construction |
| Annual operating licence | Oregon Health Authority | ORS 446.310–446.350, OAR 333-031 |
| Road approach permit | County Road Dept / ODOT | — |

**Timeline: 9–15 months. Soft costs: $55,000–$120,000.** Retain Oregon land-use counsel from day one; a LUBA appeal adds 6–12 months and is the most common cause of failure.

**Transient room tax: 8%** of gross rent in unincorporated Deschutes County (outside Bend, Redmond, Sisters and La Pine city limits). It is a collected pass-through, not revenue — but taxable "rent" includes non-optional pet, extra-vehicle, parking and recreation fees, so it must be handled correctly from the first booking.
